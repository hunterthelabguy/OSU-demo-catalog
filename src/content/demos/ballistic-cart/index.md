## Physics

The cart demonstrates relative motion and Newton's first law. A ball is loaded
onto a vertical spring inside the cart. If the spring is released while the
cart is rolling, the ball leaves with the same horizontal velocity as the
cart, and to a good approximation no horizontal force acts differently on
ball and cart while the ball is in flight (ground and air friction are small).
Both keep the same horizontal velocity, so the ball comes down into the moving
cart.

## Setup

1. Place the ball inside the top chute of the cart.
2. Hold the metal pin in one hand. With the other, push the ball down to load
   the spring, then insert the pin into the two small circular holes on the
   side of the chute before letting go. It may not go in all the way, but it
   should lock the spring.
3. Set the cart somewhere with an unblocked, straight path on a smooth
   surface. Avoid carpet and other high-friction floors.

## Procedure

1. Pose the prediction prompt and collect answers.
2. Give the cart a short, hard push. Right after you stop pushing, pull the
   pin out with the string. Do not keep pushing after the launch, or you will
   accelerate the cart away from the ball, and do not yank the pin sideways,
   which can disturb the cart's path.
3. The ball shoots up and lands back in the moving cart. A faster cart reads
   better from the back of the room.

## Quirks and caveats

This demonstration is difficult to run cold; practice before class until the
catch is consistent. Do not substitute balls of a different size, especially
larger ones, which jam in the chute.

## Pedagogical notes

Run predict-first. The wrong answers are exactly the target misconceptions,
and the discussion after the catch is where the idea that the ball carries the
cart's horizontal velocity gets consolidated. The transfer case is dropping an
object inside a moving vehicle.
